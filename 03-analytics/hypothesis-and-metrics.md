New value: Riverty moves from a checkout option Lena doesn't prefer to an everyday card she carries. Riverty's banking licence and Mastercard platform are already in place, and a consumer card is the next product that can earn that investment. What the research says about each outcome
Retention. This is the strongest link. In 2022 about 1.64m of roughly 3.16m unique users made a single Riverty transaction, so the real retention problem sits mostly outside the card's reach. The card targets users who already come back: about 318k heavy and 193k hardcore users, and roughly 82k heavy users in the app. Heavy and hardcore users grew about 25% to 30% in 2022 per the deck, which suggests this group is worth protecting.
Churn. There are two kinds to track. User churn means a heavy user drifts to Klarna or PayPal, and the card should slow that. Card churn means a cardholder stops using the card, and the business case assumes 5% a year. Neither rate has a baseline in the research, so we can't yet say how much the card changes either.
Revenue. Direct card revenue is thin. Using the deck's ball-park interchange of 0.1% on the case's volumes, I get roughly EUR 125k in year one (EUR 125m) and about EUR 1.0m in year five (EUR 1.02bn). Compare that with the deck's EUR 1.4m to 1.8m build cost. This is my own arithmetic on estimates, but it shows interchange alone won't carry the case. The real revenue upside comes from more transactions per user, conversion to Flex, and adjacent services, and none of those were validated with users.
Where the numbers don't line up
Segment size. The case's 31,238 active cards imply a base of about 1.05m users at 5% × 85% × 70%. The deck's sweet-spot segment is about 82k heavy app users, which would give only about 2,400 active cards at the same rates. Even all 511k heavy and hardcore users would give about 15,000. Either the model includes lighter users, or the target segment is far smaller than the model assumes.
The 5% input. The fake-door result was 5% clicking "Keep me informed," which is a weak signal and a different measure from conversion.
Margin. The deck's estimate takes contribution margin from about 1.3% to about -0.1%, so more volume through the card does not by itself improve profit.
What I'd measure

Leading indicators (weeks to months): share of wallet, card transactions per user, return-handled-in-app rate, app sessions, and autopay adoption.
Lagging indicators (quarters): 12-month retention of cardholders against a matched group of non-cardholder heavy users, churn to competitors, and revenue per user.
Strategic KPIs from the deck: number of active users, number of actions, customer lifetime, and revenue per action.

A matched comparison group is what separates card-driven retention from the fact that heavy users were already loyal. Without it, you'd risk crediting the card for behaviour that was already there.

Caveats
No baselines. The research gives no retention or churn rates for heavy users, so the direction is credible but the size is unknown.
Selection bias. Users who choose a card are likely already the most engaged, which could flatter early retention numbers.
Evidence strength. Provider-switching and forgetting to pay are well supported in interviews. Share-of-wallet shift and Flex conversion are assumptions to test in the friends-and-family pilot.

Sample size. A friends-and-family pilot will be too small for statistical significance, so use it for directional evidence and to find problems, not to prove an effect. The fake door exposed 49,550 users, which is a better sizing reference for any later test.
The actual numbers. I can't responsibly set the thresholds for off-network use, retention uplift or margin without baselines. Set them in Gate 0, after you've measured current heavy-user behaviour.
Time pressure. The deck estimates 3 months of team setup, 6 to 9 months of MVP build and at least 6 months for a card issuer to launch. Those can overlap, but they mean Gate 1 can't start until the pilot build is done.
One rule to apply

Agree the thresholds and kill criteria before the pilot starts, and write them down. Otherwise a weak result tends to get reinterpreted, which is the drift the brief warns about when sequencing defaults to whichever workstream pushes hardest.
