# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Lena, 32, Cologne. Lena is salaried on a middling income with no real financial cushion. She shops almost entirely on her phone and uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases. She tells us it isn't because she can't afford them: she prefers the money to leave her account only after she has decided to keep the item. She has Klarna and PayPal installed and uses whichever option appears at checkout. She knows Riverty, but doesn't prefer it and rarely goes looking for it.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To pay only after she has decided to keep an item, wherever she buys it.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** She can't use Riverty at every merchant online and in store. So she uses Klarna, PayPal, Debit or Credit Cards.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Klarna and PayPal, which she already has installed and uses whenever Riverty isn't offered. Debit or credit cards as the fallback in stores and at merchants with no BNPL. In practice she also leans on informal tracking tools such as memory, notes or receipts for return dates (this part is a hypothesis drawn from other interviewees, not confirmed for Lena).
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** She picks an item on her phone and reaches checkout (or the till in a store).
She takes whichever pay-later option appears, usually Klarna or PayPal, or falls back on her debit or credit card if there is none.
She decides whether to keep the item, often by ordering two sizes and sending one back.
She keeps the return window and due dates in her head or on paper.
She waits for the refund to show and checks that she doesn't still owe anything.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment of misery is at the till or non-partner checkout, when she has already decided she wants the item but finds no pay-later option. She taps her debit card, the money leaves her account before she has decided to keep it, and she is left tracking the return herself, which is the opposite of what she wants.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Lena (UXR-01, synthetic): "If a shop only takes card, I just don't buy as much." She also described ordering two sizes and sending one back because she doesn't pay until she has decided.
Sabine (UXR-03, synthetic): "I just tap my debit card and then it's gone from my account. That's the thing I hate."
Franziska (UXR-09, synthetic): she left a shop with no pay-later, bought the helmet online on the pavement, and paid an extra delivery fee.
Brief: Lena has Klarna and PayPal installed and uses whichever appears at checkout.
