# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Lena, the Decide-First Juggler

Role: A salaried, phone-first online shopper who spreads BNPL across Klarna, PayPal and Riverty, using whichever appears at checkout, and who often orders items she may send back.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Goal: To receive and inspect what she bought before any money leaves her account, while keeping a clear picture of what she owes and when, so she never misses a payment or pays reminder fees.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Friction (Moment of Misery): She is forced to abandon "decide first, pay later" whenever Riverty isn't offered, either switching to Klarna or PayPal or paying upfront by debit card and waiting for a refund. Meanwhile her open payments sit across several apps with different due dates and return rules, so she risks losing track and forgetting to pay.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** External tools she relies on
PayPal: the preferred online method for most interviewees, valued for its 30-day deferral and as a peer-to-peer tool. Observed
Klarna (app and invoice): used for reminders, transparency, and its return handling, where payment is paused until the return is confirmed. Observed
Debit card, bank app or phone wallet: the direct-payment fallback. Observed
Budget book or budget apps (for example Monkey, finanzguru): used by several interviewees to track spending, though one said categorising by hand was "too much work." Observed
Calendar, notes or reminders for due dates and return windows: not mentioned in the research. Inferred
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Her steps today
She sees which option checkout offers. She picks whichever BNPL provider appears and doesn't search for Riverty. Observed (UXR-03, UXR-04)
If Riverty isn't offered, she switches. She uses Klarna or PayPal, or pays upfront with her debit card. Observed
If she pays upfront, she waits for the refund. The deck cites a 3-4 bank day wait for refunds, and Klarna was valued for avoiding "back and forth payments." Partly observed
She tracks what she owes across providers. She relies on each provider's reminders, plus her bank app and, for some, a budget book or app. A calendar or spreadsheet is Inferred.
She checks each provider's return rule when sending something back. Klarna freezes the due date once a return starts, while others vary. Partly observed (UXR-17)
She pays each provider separately, and transfers money herself or relies on automatic debit. Inferred
She reconciles spending by hand, moving data between apps or categorising it herself. Partly observed
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Core frustration

Lena can't count on "decide first, pay later" being available when she needs it. Her protection is tied to whichever provider the merchant happens to show, so she has to re-negotiate how she pays on every purchase, and she is never sure where her money, her return and her due date stand.

The exact moment it feels most broken

The moment of return. She has already paid through some provider, or upfront by card, and she now sends the item back, with no clear answer on whether she still owes money, when the refund arrives, or which provider's due date applies.

Why this is the breaking point, not the checkout:

It is her reason for using BNPL. The interviews call returns "the reason to use BNPL in first place" (UXR-17). This is the moment the promise either holds or doesn't.
The failure is most visible here. If Riverty wasn't offered and she paid by debit card, she waits for the refund (the deck cites 3-4 bank days). If she used a BNPL provider, she depends on that provider's own rules. Klarna freezes the due date once a return starts, which interviewees praised (UXR-17), and other providers vary.
It collides with the juggling. A return is a state change across several apps with different due dates, so this is where she is most likely to lose track or forget a payment (UXR-09, UXR-12).
It's where her trust is won or lost. Online return timing and invoice adjustment were described as unclear, and users said the sooner the better.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Evidence that returns are why she uses BNPL
"Very important (reason to use BNPL in first place)." (Returns feature evaluation, interview notes)
"Uses Klarna because of returns (no back and forth payments), security reasons (receiving order than paying)..." (Understanding BNPL)
"Usage because of returns and security (don't know seller, receive items first)." (Understanding BNPL)
"Since possible returns trigger the use of BNPL, easy financial processing is welcome." (Feature rating slide)
"Deferred payment is an advantage esp. when buying clothing (which is most at risk of a return)." (Reactions to the value proposition)
Evidence that the return process is where it can break
"Usually return processes are very intransparent." (Returns feature evaluation)
"It's not entirely clear when exactly the money will be returned or when the invoice will be adjusted in the case of online purchases. The sooner the better is the rule." (Returns feature evaluation)
"Online usually you have to wait until return is confirmed, but if she would get back money automatically would be nice to save time and effort." (Returns feature evaluation)
"Don't like pending status." The same participant had "many open questions (partial/online returns... payment 'frozen', automatically payback)." (Returns feature evaluation)
Unanswered questions in the summary: "How about online/partial returns?" and "Where to specify return: in shop/app of seller or in Riverty app?" (Concept – Returns summary)
Evidence of what a working return looks like (the Klarna benchmark)
"Likes Klarnas procedure (as soon as a return is started, the payment due date is 'frozen'. No payment needed until return is confirmed)." (Returns feature evaluation)
"Works already great with Klarna (pause payment until merchant receive return). Should be handled the same." (Returns feature evaluation)
"Payment should be paused until return was confirmed." (Returns feature evaluation)
Listed as a requested feature: "'Pause' payment while return is not confirmed." (Overall impression summary)
Evidence for the debit-card-and-wait behaviour
Prefers invoice "because of returns and security reasons" and says it "gives her security: goods arrive and money is not gone." (Current payment habits and Understanding BNPL)
The refund wait: the deck's returns concept slide describes the pain it solves as "No more waiting 3-4 bank days for return to be paid back." This is Riverty's own prototype wording, not a user statement, so it shows the team's view of the problem, not a participant's experience.
Evidence of losing track
"Do not want to forget to pay (happened before)." (Understanding BNPL)
"One more payment option is one too much. Lose track of spendings." (Prototype first impressions)
Klarna's appeal: "gets reminded to pay" and "payment reminders" from its app. (Understanding BNPL)
How strong this evidence is

Well supported by the research:

Returns are a core reason users choose BNPL.
Online return handling is unclear on timing, and users want payment paused until a return is confirmed.
Klarna's pause is the benchmark users name.
Some users have forgotten to pay before.
